# 6 Common Cancer Screening Challenges and How to Overcome Them

*Written by Elaine Ho*

Cancer screening can help detect certain cancers early, sometimes before symptoms appear. Yet knowing that screening is important does not always make it easy to take the next step.

Fear of the results, financial concerns, limited access to services, or simply not knowing when to get screened can all stand in the way. For some people, embarrassment or difficulty understanding health information can make the process even more challenging.

These barriers are common, but they do not have to stop you from seeking care.

Here are six challenges people may face when considering cancer screening and practical ways to move forward.

## 1. Fear of the Unknown: “What if they find something?”

For many people, the thought of cancer screening brings anxiety. You may worry about the procedure, what the results might show, or what could happen next.

There is even a term for the anxiety people may experience around medical scans and waiting for results: [scanxiety](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/latest-news/how-to-cope-with-scanxiety.html). Sometimes, the uncertainty can feel so overwhelming that delaying screening seems easier.

Yet screening can detect certain cancers before symptoms develop, when there may be more treatment options available.

**What can help:** Learn what to expect before your appointment. Ask how the screening works, what happens at the screening, how to prepare, when you will receive your results, and what happens afterwards. Having clear information can help you feel more prepared and make the unknown feel less intimidating.

## 2. The Cost of Getting Screened: “Can I afford it?”

The cost of screening can extend beyond the test itself. Transportation, childcare, time away from work, and follow-up appointments can all add up, making screening difficult to prioritize.

**What can help:** Ask about possible costs and available support before your appointment. In Ontario, government-funded health services, community health centres, and nonprofit organizations may offer free or reduced-cost screening or other forms of assistance. In Ghana, public health facilities, community health programmes, and nonprofit organizations may also provide free or reduced-cost screening or support. Asking about available options in advance can help you understand what is covered and what support may be available to you. Organizations like the [**Alliance for CancerCare Equity (ACCE)**](/who-we-are) can also help. We support patients and families in Canada and Ghana by reducing financial barriers and making cancer care more accessible.

## 3. Knowing When to Get Screened: “Which screenings do I need?”

With different recommendations for breast, cervical, colorectal, lung, and other cancers, it is understandable to feel unsure about where to begin.

There is no single screening schedule that is right for everyone. Screening recommendations may vary depending on your age, health history, family history, and individual risk factors.

**What can help:** You do not need to navigate every recommendation on your own. A conversation with a healthcare professional about the screening options and recommended schedules available to you can be a good place to start.

Try asking:

**“Are there any cancer screenings I should be getting right now?”**

One simple question can help you understand what is appropriate for you.

## 4. Limited Access: “What if screening is out of reach?”

For many people, the challenge is not whether they want to be screened, but whether they can access screening at all.

People living in rural or underserved communities may need to travel long distances. Others may face transportation difficulties, limited appointment availability, or shortages of healthcare providers.

**What can help:** Look for community clinics, mobile screening programs, or local screening events. Community health centres and cancer organizations may also be able to connect you with nearby services or transportation support.

## 5. Feeling Uncomfortable or Embarrassed

Some cancer screenings can feel personal or uncomfortable. Concerns about privacy, the procedure itself, or being examined may make people hesitant to book an appointment.

These feelings are understandable, and your comfort matters.

**What can help:** Talk openly with your healthcare provider about your concerns and ask them to explain the procedure beforehand. Depending on the screening and services available, you may also be able to request a healthcare provider of a particular gender.

## 6. When Health Information Is Hard to Understand

Cancer screening information is not always easy to navigate.

Medical terminology, complicated instructions, unfamiliar healthcare systems, and language barriers can make it difficult to understand which screenings you need or what steps to take. Some people may also assume that screening is unnecessary when they feel healthy, even though screening is designed to detect certain cancers before symptoms appear.

**What can help:** Ask your healthcare provider to explain information in plain language. You can also ask about translated materials or interpretation services when available.
