# What Everyone Should Know About Liver Cancer

*Written by Seyitan Moritiwon*

The liver is a vital organ that plays an essential role in keeping our bodies healthy. It processes nutrients, filters harmful substances from the blood, and produces bile to help digest fats. When the liver stops functioning properly, it can lead to serious health problems, and severe liver failure may require a transplant. Like many other organs, the liver can also develop cancer.

Liver cancer may start out as a small lump, and can develop in more than one area in the liver over time. It starts slowly in a liver that has been exposed to certain risk factors for liver cancer.

The tumours are often discovered during routine medical tests, even before any symptoms appear. However, symptoms include discomfort or pain in the abdomen, fatigue, or swelling. Healthcare providers often use blood tests, imaging scans, and biopsies to detect a tumour and guide treatment decisions.

## There are two kinds of liver cancer

- **Primary liver cancer:** This kind of cancer starts in the liver. The most common type is [hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/hepatocellular-carcinoma/symptoms-causes/syc-20589101), which develops in the liver's main cells. Other, less common types can develop in different cells within the liver.
- **[Secondary (metastatic) liver cancer](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/metastatic-liver-cancer):** This type begins in another part of the body, such as the colon, breast, or lungs, and spreads to the liver. It is more common than primary liver cancer, and treatment usually depends on where the cancer originally started.

## Causes of liver cancer

There is no single cause of liver cancer. However, certain infections, health conditions and lifestyle or environmental factors can increase a person's risk. Some risk factors include:

- **Hepatitis B/D or C infections:** Chronic infection causes liver inflammation which can lead to cirrhosis and cancer.
- **Cirrhosis:** Scarring from hepatitis, heavy alcohol use, or other chronic liver conditions increases the risk of developing liver cancer.
- **Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH):** MASLD occurs when excess fat builds up in the liver, while MASH is a more severe form that causes inflammation and may lead to scarring, increasing the risk of liver cancer.
- **Heavy alcohol use:** Long-term drinking can lead to cirrhosis and cancer. Individuals with alcohol-related liver disease are more likely to develop primary liver cancer.
- **Smoking:** One of the functions of the liver is to filter the blood. Smoking increases exposure to harmful chemicals that damage the liver and can cause liver cancer.
- **Exposure to harmful chemicals:** Certain toxic substances, like aflatoxins, or industrial chemicals can damage the liver and increase the risk of developing cancer.
- **Genetic conditions:** Rare diseases, like hemochromatosis, can harm the liver over time and increase the risk of developing liver cancer.

## What are the warning signs of liver cancer?

Liver cancer may not cause any symptoms in its early stages. As it progresses, patients might notice symptoms such as: pain in the upper right side of abdomen or in the back and shoulder, fluid buildup in abdomen, fatigue and weakness, mental confusion or disorientation, itchiness, edema, loss of appetite, nausea, dark urine and jaundice.

## The cure for liver cancer

Successful liver transplants can cure liver cancer. However not everyone who needs a liver transplant can receive one, as they may not be healthy enough to go through the process.

## Preventing liver cancer

Like many other cancers, you may not be able to prevent liver cancer. But taking the following steps may reduce your risk:

- Get a hepatitis B shot.
- Avoid contact with hepatitis C infected blood and don't share needles or syringes.
- Quit smoking.
- Reduce or stop drinking alcohol.
- Reach or maintain a weight that's right for you.
- Ask your provider about liver cancer screening. Regular blood tests and liver ultrasounds may make sense if you have liver disease, diabetes or obesity.

## Awareness matters

It is crucial that everyone is aware of liver cancer but it is particularly important in communities where chronic hepatitis B is common and access to testing and treatment may be limited. Without awareness people will not know they're living with hepatitis, underestimate their risk or delay seeking medical attention when symptoms appear.
