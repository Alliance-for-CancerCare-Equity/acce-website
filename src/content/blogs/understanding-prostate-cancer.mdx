# Aware, Not Afraid: Understanding Prostate Cancer

*Written by Nusaiba Mohammed*

It is easy to go too far in either direction when it comes to our health.

Sometimes we worry about every little change. A headache makes us wonder if something is wrong. Feeling tired makes us start thinking about every possible reason.

Other times, we do the opposite. We notice something different and tell ourselves that it is probably nothing.

Neither one is really helpful.

We don't need to spend our lives worrying about getting sick. But we should also pay attention to our health and notice when something changes.

## You Don't Have to Be Afraid of Your Health

Hearing the word cancer can be scary.

It can make us think about the worst possible outcome before we even know what is happening.

But being aware of our health doesn't mean being afraid.

A change in your body does not automatically mean that you have cancer. There can be many different reasons for the symptoms we experience.

For example, changes in urination are common as men get older and can happen for reasons other than prostate cancer.

The point is not to assume the worst.

The point is to know when something is different and get the right information.

## Know What Is Normal for You

We all have different bodies and different routines.

You might normally sleep through the night but suddenly start waking up several times to use the bathroom. You might notice that urinating takes longer than it used to, or that your urine stream has become weaker.

These changes can have many possible causes. But if something is new, continues, or concerns you, it is worth mentioning to a healthcare provider.

Other symptoms that can occur with prostate cancer include blood in the urine or semen, difficulty starting or stopping urination, and pain when urinating or ejaculating. Prostate cancer may also have no symptoms in its early stages.

The important thing is, you should know your own normal.

## Regular Checkups Are Part of Taking Care of Yourself

Sometimes we only think about seeing a healthcare provider when something is wrong.

But regular appointments can also be a chance to talk about your health before there is a problem.

For prostate cancer, screening may include a [PSA blood test](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/psa-test/about/pac-20384731) and, in some situations, a [digital rectal exam](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/24212-digital-rectal-exam). A PSA test measures the amount of prostate-specific antigen in the blood, but a higher PSA level does not automatically mean someone has cancer. Other conditions can also increase PSA levels.

## Your Risk May Be Different From Someone Else's

Not everyone has the same risk of prostate cancer.

Age is an important factor, but family history can also matter. Having a father, brother or son who has had prostate cancer can increase a man's risk. Black men also have a higher risk of developing prostate cancer.

This is one reason why it is useful to know your family history and talk openly with your healthcare provider.

## Don't Turn Awareness Into Anxiety

There is a difference between paying attention to your health and constantly worrying about it.

Being aware means noticing when something has changed.

Being anxious about your health can mean constantly looking for something to be wrong.

We don't need to do that.

Notice it.

Write it down if you need to.

Talk to a healthcare provider.

Find out what is actually happening.

Sometimes the answer will be something simple. Sometimes it may require more testing. Either way, knowing is better than spending days imagining every possible explanation.

## A Final Thought

We should enjoy our lives, make plans, spend time with the people we care about and not let every small symptom become something to worry about.

At the same time, we shouldn't ignore symptoms.

There is a middle ground.

Pay attention to your body. Go for regular checkups. Know your family history. Ask questions when something changes.
